About us

Why Shari Lagbe? exists

Home / About

Started with one question

"Shari lagbe?" — do you need a shari? — is what weaving families in Tangail have asked traders at their doorstep for generations, often for a fraction of what the same saree sells for in the city.

We built this site to close that gap: to let the weaver set a fair price, and let the buyer see exactly whose hands made what they're wearing.

Every saree here is listed with the weaver's village, the yarn used, and roughly how many days it took on the loom — because that context is part of what you're paying for.

What we stand for

Three things we don't compromise on

Fair to the weaver

We agree a price with the weaver before the saree is even finished — never after, and never below their asking rate.

Honest listings

No stock photos of sarees we don't have. What's photographed is what ships, flaws in the weave included.

Real delivery windows

Handloom takes time. We tell you the honest wait instead of a date we'll have to apologise for later.

How we got here

A short timeline

2024

First trip to Pathrail

Spent a week talking to weaving families about what a fair online marketplace would actually need to get right.

2025

First 20 sarees listed

Started small, with a handful of Jamdani and cotton pieces from five weaving families around Tangail.

2026

Site rebuilt from scratch

Rebuilt the shopping experience end to end — this version — with cart, filters, and a design language drawn from the saree border itself.
